Part One: Reproduction according to the caliber of the paper

According to the paper's method, without deducting costs and closing transactions at the end of the month, the Sharpe value since 2006 has been 0.61 and the t value has been 2.76. The momentum effect exists significantly, but it is weaker than the paper's and is greater than 1.

  1. Signal: At the end of each month, look at the cumulative returns of the past 12 months. If it is positive, go long, if it is negative, go short.
  2. Volatility: exponentially weighted variance of daily returns, central mass 60 days, annualized coefficient 243 (261 used in the paper).
  3. Position: 40% ÷ annualized volatility, making the risk contribution of each instrument similar.
  4. Portfolio: Instruments can be traded with equal weights in the current month and held for 1 month; the average trading volume of instruments in the past 60 days must be ≥ 1,000 lots.

The trend in China is shorter than overseas: the lookback period of 1 to 9 months is significantly better than the 12 months used in the paper.

Year-by-year performance

Crisis protection still exists in the real version; losses are concentrated in years when the market reverses rapidly (such as the V-shaped rebound in 2009 and the supply-side reform in 2016). For comparison, the always long portfolio lost 49% in 2008 and 30% in 2014.

Part Two: Real Offer Settings

The paper does not deduct costs, does not consider capital scale and execution details; the following conditions are added on the premise that signals and position rules remain unchanged. Only the impact coefficient and the upper capacity limit remain empirical parameters.

Conclusion

  1. The effect exists, but the actual benefits are limited.About half of the Sharpe is eaten up by execution conditions and costs. The actual Sharpe is about 0.3, and it is even weaker after 2016.
  2. The three most influential links: Transaction time point (−0.08), impact cost (−0.07), month-to-month cost (−0.05).
  3. Contract rolls is the largest source of cost, annualized at 1.48%, mainly from non-ferrous metals that change from month to month.
  4. Advantages of short lookback period offset by costs: 1~9 months is the best when there is no friction, and k = 6 is the best after deducting costs.
  5. Limited capacity: Funding increased from 100 million to 1 billion, and Sharpe dropped from 0.31 to 0.20.
  6. Crisis protection is still there: During the unilateral crashes in 2008 and 2014, the real version also made 10% and 35% respectively.

Limitations and directions for improvement

  • Impact coefficient 0.5 has not been calibrated to actual data.
  • The price difference is estimated from the minute transaction price by the Roll method, which only guarantees the order of magnitude; the original data does not have a bid or ask quote.
  • It does not take into account the inability to complete transactions at the price limit, the exchange's position limit, and the temporary increase in margin.
  • The handling fee is uniformly 1bp.
  1. Optimize month swaps: Non-ferrous metals hold deferred-contract contracts to reduce the number of month swaps; use inter-temporal spread orders to swap months.
  2. Reduce invalid transactions: Do not adjust positions when there is little change; put month changes and position adjustments on the same day.
  3. Multi-look period combination: average signals over 3, 6, and 12 months.
  4. Improving risk allocation; predicting volatility with HAR models (see research/har_rv）。
  5. Overlay Carry signal.
