Intraday Momentum Recurrence Report

Reproduction paper: Lei Gao, Yufeng Han, Sophia Zhengzi Li & Guofu Zhou，《Market Intraday Momentum》，Journal of Financial Economics, 2018 · Main sample: CSI 300 stock index futures (corresponding to the SPY of the paper); supplementary: SSE 50, CSI 500, CSI 1000, Treasury bond futures, 23 commodity futures · 1 minute data

Intraday momentum exists on China futures, butThe half hour that drove it was different from the paper. The most important thing in the paper is the return r1 in the first half hour after the opening (including overnight); on CSI 300, r1 has only a marginal effect (t = 1.86, out-of-sample R² is negative). What is really predictive is the r12 return in the penultimate half-hour before the close (t = 2.70, out-of-sample R² 1.44%), which is almost identical to the paper’s r12 result. This is true for the four stock index futures; the reverse is true for Treasury bond futures, with r1 being significant and r12 not significant; and commodity futures r1 and r12 both being generally positive. Higher volatility, higher volume, and greater predictability during the 2015 crash are consistent with the paper. The gross return of signal timing is statistically significant, but only a few basis points per trade: after deducting the one-tick spread, the stock index is still surplus, most commodities are insufficient, and the stock index also faces higher intraday trading fees.

What does the paper talk about

Cut a trading day into 13 half-hours. The paper found that: the return in the first half-hour after the opening (r1, calculated from the previous day's close, including overnight information) can positively predict the return in the last half-hour of the day (r13); the return in the penultimate half-hour (r12) also has predictive power and is complementary to r1. The paper gives two explanations: First, informed traders complete position adjustments before the market closes, and the direction is consistent with the information digested at the opening; second, investors who do not often trade (such as institutions that need the closing price) place orders intensively in the late trading. Predictability is stronger during periods of high volatility, high volume, and financial crises, and can translate into substantial timing gains.

Conclusion

  • Intraday momentum exists: Across stock indexes, Treasury bonds, and most commodity futures, earlier returns of the day are positively predictive of the last half hour; predictability is concentrated in high volatility, high volume, and crisis periods, consistent with the paper.
  • But the core variable r1 of the paper does not hold true on the Chinese stock index.: The predictive power of stock index futures comes from r12 (one hour to half an hour before the close), r1 has only a marginal effect and out-of-sample is negative. Treasury bond futures are dominated by r1. Therefore, in the A-share stock index, there is insufficient evidence for "the opening information continues to the closing", and "the trading direction continues in the late trading" is more consistent with the data.
  • Limited economic value: Gross returns are significant, but only a few basis points per trade. The profit of the commodity is eaten up by the price difference; the stock index is still positive after deducting the price difference, but it still has to face intraday trading fees.
