China futures volatility roughness (Rough Volatility) recurrence report

Reproduction paper: Jim Gatheral, Thibault Jaisson & Mathieu Rosenbaum，《Volatility is Rough》，Quantitative Finance, 2018 · 8 instruments, sample to 2026-09 · Only futures data (no options)

The paper’s conclusions largely carry over to Chinese futures: log volatility resembles fractional Brownian motion with Hurst exponent H≈0.1, far rougher than Brownian motion (H=0.5). The one-parameter RFSV forecast outperforms HAR and AR at 5- and 20-day horizons. The apparent long memory (d≈0.5–0.6) in the HAR study can be explained by rough volatility. Unlike the paper, Chinese futures volatility increments are clearly heavy-tailed.

2. Single fractal scale is established

When q ≤ 2, ζ_q/q is almost unchanged (single fractal); when q = 3, it is slightly lower, most obvious for copper, gold, and PTA. This is the effect of extreme values (fat tail), see the next section.

In the HAR study, it was estimated that d = 0.47~0.64, partially exceeding the stationary boundary of 0.5. At that time, explanations of structural changes such as night trading were excluded. Here is the answer: an RFSV process without long memory and H≈0.1, when estimated by the same method, d will be around 0.55~0.60. The so-called "stronger long memory than overseas" is mainly a statistical artifact caused by rough volatility. This also explains why HAR, which only looks at information within 22 days, is on par with ARFIMA.

Differences from the paper

  • The paper uses the realized variance of the stock index of the Oxford-Man library; here the main continuous 1-minute and 5-minute returns of the futures are used.
  • Choose 10:30–11:29 for the 1-hour window (avoiding the opening and lunch breaks); all-day RV includes night trading and skips market breaks.
  • Kernel integrals predicted by RFSV are truncated at 500 days and normalized; H is estimated using the full sample (same paper, slightly favorable for RFSV).
  • There is no option data, and the subsequent parts of the paper about the implied volatility surface (rBergomi, rough Heston) have not been done.
