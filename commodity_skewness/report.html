Commodity Futures Skewness Premium Recurrence Report

Reproduction paper: Adrian Fernandez-Perez, Bart Frijns, Ana-Maria Fuertes & Joëlle Miffre，《The Skewness of Commodity Futures Returns》，Journal of Banking & Finance, 2018 · China Commodity Futures, 2006-06 to 2026-08, 239 months, 73 instruments

China Commodity FuturesNo skewness premium. According to the paper's method of shorting positive skewness and longing negative skewness instruments, the annualized long-short return is -1.48% (t = -0.78), and the paper's is 8.01% (t = 3.83); the α for the benchmark model is 0.86% (t = 0.42), and the paper's is 6.58% (t = 3.58). The reason is that China's skewness and momentum are in opposite directions: instruments with positive skewness were precisely the winners in the past (the average daily return of Q5 in the past 12 months was 6.6%, and Q1 was -6.6%), while the instruments with negative skewness in the paper are the winners and discounted instruments. After excluding the influence of factors such as momentum and then calculating skewness, α becomes 4.74% (t = 2.54), indicating that the skewness premium is masked by momentum, but it is not strong in itself.

What does the paper talk about

Sorting commodities by the skewness of their daily returns in the past 12 months, the quintile with the lowest long skewness (left skew) and the highest short skewness (right skew, like lottery tickets), the annualized rate of 27 commodities in the United States from 1987 to 2014 was 8.01%, with a Sharpe of 0.78. After controlling for the term structure, momentum, and hedging pressure, α is still 6.58%. The explanation is that investors (including industrial clients who do "selective hedging") prefer right-skewed "lottery-type" assets, making them overvalued and causing low future returns. The risk price of the skewness factor in the cross-sectional test is significantly positive.

The biggest difference from the paper: The left-skewed Q1 in the paper is an instrument with a premium and good past performance, while the right-skewed Q5 is a contango with an instrument with poor past performance. The skewness is in the same direction as momentum and term structure. In China, instruments that have risen much are often pulled up by sharp increases in a few days, so they skew to the right; this makes "short right skew" equal to "short the winner" in China.

  • Skewness after filtering: Use the daily returns in the past 12 months to regress the three factors of EW, TS, and Mom, take the residuals to calculate the skewness, and then sort. Under the paper rules, α rises to 4.7% (t = 2.5). In other words, after removing the "momentum component of skewness", the skewness premium does exist. But its raw long-short return is only about 0.8%, with alpha largely coming from the negative exposure to momentum being clawed back. It is not significant under the dominant contract standard.
  • Neither is significant after controlling only for calendar month (seasonality) or after excluding the least liquid 20%.
  • Replacing the enhanced model (adding liquidity, position changes, value, and volatility) does not change the conclusion.
  • Period-wise: the direction of the skewness premium becomes positive after 2016 (paper rule α 4.3%, t = 1.85), and negative before 2016.
  • Break-even cost: The paper is 0.93%; China’s long-short returns themselves are negative or very small, and there is no break-even cost.

7. Why did 2018 flip around?

The same four-part grouping is full and long (left bias − right bias, negative value = right bias outperforms). Test 1: Whether momentum is mixed into skewness - (a) Skewness is related to the cross-sectional rank of returns in the past 12 months; (b) First divide into two groups according to momentum, and go long and short according to skewness within the group; (c) Do a cross-sectional regression of skewness on momentum every month, and use the residuals to sort; (d) Fama-MacBeth: Next month's return simultaneously regresses skewness and momentum (cross-sectional standardization), and the coefficient is "skewness is high 1 standard deviation, how much higher is next month’s return” (annualized %). Test 2: Look at it year by year.

  • The “reverse skewness effect” before 2018 was momentum: Skewness is positively correlated with past returns (rank correlation about 0.2~0.3), and momentum was strong during this period. The effect is halved and insignificant after sorting within the momentum group, close to 0 after sorting with residuals, and the skewness coefficient also becomes 0 after Fama-MacBeth controls for momentum (t ≈ 0.1). That is, there is no independent skewness effect then.
  • An independent, U.S.-directed skewness premium emerged after 2018: The original ranking cannot be seen (+3% ~ +4%, not significant), because the right-skewed instrument is still biased towards the winner and the momentum is still strong; but after controlling the momentum, the right-skewed instrument significantly underperforms - the residual ranking under the no-threshold caliber is +11.3% (t = 2.27), the Fama-MacBeth coefficient is −4.9% (t = −3.14); the position threshold caliber direction is the same, and the significance is weak (t ≈ −1.8).
  • It’s not a clean one-time flip on a year-to-year basis.: The annual numbers are very noisy, and the residual skewness is positive in 2016–2019, 2021, and 2025 and negative in 2020, 2022, and 2024. Conclusions after 2018 should be considered prima facie.
  • Why the independent skewness premium only appeared after 2018 was not tested in this study; it may be related to the listing of commodity options (starting in 2017 and massive expansion after 2019) and the increase in institutional participation, which is only speculation.

Conclusion

  • The conclusion of the paper does not hold true for the entire Chinese sample: Instruments that are short right-skewed and long left-skewed are not profitable as a whole from 2006 to 2026, and the skewness factor has no pricing power in the cross-section. However, looking at periods, there was a significant “reverse” trend before 2018 (outperforming to the right, Li, Sun & Zhou 2021 also found), which is actually the shadow of momentum; after 2018, after controlling for momentum, an independent skewness premium appeared in the same direction as the United States.
  • The reason is that skewness is inversely related to momentum in China: China’s winner instruments are often driven by sharp rises in a few days (right skew), so the skewness signal becomes a “reactionary momentum” signal; only after excluding the momentum component can the skewness premium be vaguely seen. Fan & Zhang (2020, JFM) also finds that the skewness premium in China is “distorted” by margin and position limits.
  • Consistent with our previous findings: The cross-sectional returns of Chinese commodities are mainly driven by term structure (carry) and momentum, and skewness is not a new source independent of them.
