HARQ Volatility Forecast Recurrence Report

Reproduction paper: Tim Bollerslev, Andrew J. Patton & Rogier Quaedvlieg，《Exploiting the Errors: A Simple Approach for Improved Volatility Forecasting》，Journal of Econometrics, 2016 (the full text is based on the manuscript accepted by the author) · CSI 300 stock index futures (corresponding to the S&P 500 in the paper) + 22 commodity futures (corresponding to the 27 Dow component stocks in the paper) · 5-minute data

The conclusion of the paper is basically replicated on Chinese futures: after considering measurement error, the model becomes more persistent (CSI 300 persistence 0.89 → 0.96) and pays more attention to recent fluctuations; the coefficient of the measurement error term is negative and significant (82% of commodities are significant). Out-of-sample, HARQ generally outperforms HAR on commodities, especially 5-day and 22-day forecasts (losses are reduced by 2%~5% on average); according to Reality Check, HARQ significantly outperforms all benchmark models on 59% of commodities. 1-day forecasts for the CSI 300 are mixed, as are the S&P 500 in the paper.

What does the paper talk about

The realized variance (RV) calculated using 5-minute returns is only an estimate of the true volatility and is subject to measurement error. The error causes the model to underestimate the persistence of fluctuations (attenuation bias). The size of the error can be estimated by the realized quartic variance (RQ): on days when RQ is large, yesterday's RV is less reliable and you should rely less on it; on days when RQ is small, you can rely more on it. HARQ Based on HAR, let the coefficient of yesterday's RV change with √RQ: β₁,t = β₁ + β₁Q·√RQ.

The longer the forecast period, the more obvious the advantage of HARQ: for commodity 5-day and 22-day forecasts, HARQ is on average 2%~5% lower than HAR under the four window and loss combinations (for example, 22-day RW QLIKE 0.952), and the 22-day forecast of CSI 300 is about 5% lower. In the paper, HARQ also outperforms HAR in most deadlines.

4. Degree of improvement and change in measurement error (corresponding to Figure 4 of the paper)

Looking across instruments, the greater the dispersion of √RQ (the more drastic the change in measurement error), the lower the QLIKE ratio of HARQ relative to HAR (correlation -0.40), which is consistent with the findings of the paper.

Conclusion

  • Measurement error also exists in the realized variance of China futures and changes with time. The mechanism of HARQ (less reliance on yesterday when the error is large) is completely consistent in the in-sample evidence.
  • Out-of-sample, HARQ is stably better than HAR in commodity futures, and the longer the period, the greater the advantage; the short-term forecast improvement of CSI 300 is not obvious.
  • Consistent with previous HAR and rough volatility studies: the volatility persistence of Chinese futures is very strong, even stronger after accounting for measurement errors.

Differences from the paper

  • Use commodity futures in place of individual stocks as the "cross section" and CSI 300 stock index futures in place of the S&P 500.
  • China futures have lunch breaks and night trading, and intraday returns are only counted for consecutive periods, and RV does not include overnight gaps.
  • The highest proportion of predictions replaced by insanity filter is about 6% (less than 0.1% in the paper), which mainly occurs in a few instruments and models with many parameters.
  • Additional robustness to Section 4 (other RV estimators, different sampling frequencies, HAR-J/CHAR/SHAR with Q) was not done.
