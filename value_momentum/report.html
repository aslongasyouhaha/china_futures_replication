China Commodity Futures Value and Momentum Recurrence Report

Reproduction paper: Clifford S. Asness, Tobias J. Moskowitz & Lasse Heje Pedersen，《Value and Momentum Everywhere》，Journal of Finance, 2013 · Commodity futures part only · China commodity futures 2010-07 to 2026-08, 194 months · Excluding costs (same paper)

Momentum is almost as strong as thesis on China commodity futures (annualized 10.3%, t=3.27; thesis 11.5%, t=3.14), while value is completely non-existent (-1.0%, thesis 7.3%). There is a negative correlation between the two (-0.59), and the half-half combination is significant. Connected with previous research: Most of the returns of momentum overlap with carry; value is apparently ineffective because it is in the opposite direction to carry; and the half-and-half combination of value and momentum is almost independent of carry, still 3.9% after controlling for carry (t=3.09).

2. Supplement: relationship with carry (not the content of the paper)

Previous three-factor research on carry and commodities found that the 12-month momentum on the dominant contract highly overlaps with the carry over the past year. Here, carry1-12 factors are constructed in the same instrument pool and in the same month (average annualized carry in the past 12 months, weighted by ranking), and the equally weighted commodity basket and carry factor are regressed:

  • momentumThe correlation with carry is 0.52, which is only about 3.5% after controlling for carry, which is not significant and consistent with previous findings.
  • valueNegatively correlated with carry (−0.43): “cheap” instruments that have fallen much over the past 5 years tend to be at a premium. Value appears to be ineffective, partly because it is naturally the opposite of carry; controlling for carry has about a 4% positive return, but it is not significant.
  • Half and half value and momentumThe combination has almost nothing to do with carry (0.16), and it is still significant after controlling for carry (about 3.9%, t≈3.1): this is the source of income that is currently found on the dominant contract and is independent of carry.

Conclusion

  • Of the two findings of the paper on commodity futures, only one is true for China: momentum is true and has the same intensity as the paper, but value is not true.
  • Value is negatively correlated with momentum and the portfolio is more stable, which is more obvious in China.
  • Combined with carry, the main source of income for Chinese commodity futures is carry; momentum mostly overlaps with it; the combination of value and momentum provides additional income independent of carry.

Differences from the paper

  • Only one asset class, commodity futures, is reproduced, and the core selling points of the paper (common factor structure across assets, liquidity risk) cannot be tested.
  • Sample 2010–2026, about 16 years (40 years of the paper); value signals need to be on the market for 5.5 years, and there are few early instruments.
  • The spot price is approximated by the unadjusted dominant-contract price, which contains the basis difference between futures and spot, and has little impact on the price changes over the past five years.
