Hedging Premium and Liquidity Premium Recurrence Report

Recurrence paper: Wenjin Kang, K. Geert Rouwenhorst & Ke Tang，《A Tale of Two Premiums: The Role of Hedgers and Speculators in Commodity Futures Markets》，Journal of Finance, 2020 · China Commodity Futures, 2005-01-07 to 2026-09-11, 77 instruments, weekly · Use the exchange’s “top 20 member positions” to represent large investors (Option B)

China’s results and the story of the paperJust the opposite. The paper found that: Speculators trade with the trend and are eager to complete transactions. After buying, the price falls, and they pay a premium to the hedgers who provide liquidity. China’s top 20 large traders also followed the trend (coefficient of net buying and week’s return 0.21, t = 9.1), but after their purchases the pricecontinueRise: According to the net buying quintile, the highest group rose 0.11% more than the lowest group on the following days 1–4 (t = 2.29) and 0.20% more (t = 3.37) on days 5–10, with no reversal thereafter. The more large investors are net short in the long term, the lower their returns will be after two weeks (t = -2.25), which is also opposite to the direction of "hedgers pay insurance premiums" in the paper. In other words, China's large investors are not paying liquidity costs, but more like the party with better information or trend judgment. Those who pay are mainly retail traders outside the top 20. This effect was concentrated before 2016 and then weakened significantly.

What does the paper talk about

Use CFTC Weekly Positioning to separate traders into commercial (hedgers) and non-commercial (speculators). The paper found two types of premiums: ① Short-term liquidity premium: Speculators trade with the trend and are eager to close the deal. The instruments they buy will have lower returns next week and the instruments they sell will have higher returns; hedgers trade against the trend, provide liquidity, and earn this part of the income (week t ≈ 5~6.6). ② Long-term hedging premium: After smoothing the hedging pressure for 52 weeks, the more net short positions the hedger has, the higher the future income will be, which is consistent with Keynes's normal backwardation theory. The two directions are opposite, so hedging pressure cannot predict returns without distinction.

Conclusion

  • China’s big players trade like the speculators in the paper(Follow the trend),But the result is like the hedger in the paper(Making money): After they buy, the price continues in the same direction and does not reverse, which means they are not paying liquidity costs.
  • The paper’s “two premiums” framework cannot be replicated in China using this type of data.: Neither the liquidity premium paid by large investors nor the hedging premium brought by long-term net short positions can be seen. Both directions are reversed. One explanation (untested) is that China’s “top 20 members” are composed of institutions and industrial clients with more information and larger funds, while those who are really “eager to make transactions and chase the ups and downs” are retail investors outside the top 20.
  • The effect is weakening: After 2016, net buying by large investors no longer predicts returns, which is consistent with "many effects weakened after 2016" in other recurrences in this database.
  • limitations: Futures companies have a mix of various customers; the top 20 members change every week; there is no distinction between trader types. These will weaken the signal and the conclusion should be regarded as the behavior of "large players as a whole".
