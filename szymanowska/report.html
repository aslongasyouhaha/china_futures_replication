What does the paper talk about

Futures returns can be split into two parts:backwardation(The income obtained by holding the nearest contract and changing months before expiration reflects the risk compensation of spot prices) andterm premium(The income from buying a deferred-contract and selling a nearby-contract reflects the risk compensation of the term structure). The paper uses four strategies to separate them: Short Roll (continuous rolling of the nearest-by-contract n periods, only including spot premium), Holding (buying the nth contract and holding until maturity, both), Excess Holding (Holding − Short Roll, only including term premium), and Spreading (buying the spread of each term in each period and then rolling, also including only term premium).

The main findings: (1) According to basis grouping, the spot premium differs by 8% to 15% per year, and the term premium only differs by 0.6% to 1.8%, in the opposite direction; (2) Momentum, volatility, inflation beta, and liquidity grouping can also generate premiums; (3) A basis factor (Holding) Return) can explain the spot premium of almost all groups, but it cannot explain the term premium; (4) The term premium requires two other factors - the Spreading return of high basis and low basis instruments. The three factors combined describe the risk premium of commodity futures.

|α| is the average (%) of the absolute values of annualized α of the four combinations, and in parentheses is the Wald test p value when α is jointly zero, p < 0.05 bold. The basis factor in the paper explains away the spot premium of all groups (p is generally greater than 0.1), but not in China: in the basis group itself, when n = 1, the α of the P4 (high basis) portfolio reaches -7.6%. The reason is that this long-short factor has almost no correlation with the high-basis portfolio (the fluctuations in the factor mainly come from the low-basis side), while Chinese commodities as a whole rise and fall at the same time, and the equal-weighted market fluctuates greatly. After adding the equal-weighted market, R² rises from 0.18 to 0.89, α shrinks significantly, and the momentum, coefficient of variation, inflation, dollar, and hedging pressure groupings are no longer rejected when n = 1; but the basis, position, and liquidity groupings are still rejected, and most of the groupings are also rejected when n ≥ 2. In terms of term premium, the basis (holding) factor cannot explain the basis, hedging pressure, and inflation groupings (same paper); the term premiums of other groups themselves are very small and cannot be explained.

Comparison with thesis

  • consistent: Spot premium and term premium are in opposite directions, with an order of magnitude difference in magnitude; basis grouping produces significant spot premium and term premium at the same time; momentum generates spot premium but no term premium; dollar beta is invalid; the basis (Holding) factor cannot explain the term premium of basis grouping, and the two spreading factors of high/low basis are significantly better than one in most groupings.
  • inconsistent: The basis factor cannot explain the spot premium in China (the core pricing conclusion of the paper); there is no spot premium in the coefficient of variation and inflation beta grouping; the hedging pressure is in the opposite direction (but the proxy variables are different); the term premium is larger and is sensitive to deferred-contract liquidity.
  • Not inspected: Holding and Spreading grouping results in the online appendix of the paper (already calculated in output/sorts.parquet), principal component analysis of three factors, and Spreading alternative factor in Table VII.
