China Futures Volatility Management Portfolio Recurrence Report

Reproduction paper: Alan Moreira & Tyler Muir，《Volatility-Managed Portfolios》，Journal of Finance, 2017 · China stock index, treasury bonds, commodity futures and TSMOM, carry factors, sample to 2026-08 · Paper standard (excluding costs)

On the Chinese stock index, the conclusion of the paper holds: adjusting positions inversely by the variance of the previous month, the α of the CSI 300 is 6.4% per year (t=2.35), and the Sharpe rises from 0.40 to 0.60, which is equivalent to the paper's US stock market. On commodities it is ineffective or even harmful: the average commodity factor has an alpha of -3.1%. Taking it apart, the mechanism of the paper (reduce positions when short-term fluctuations suddenly amplify) is very effective in stock indexes. When the CSI 500 only uses this part, α reaches 11%; commodities lose money in another part: periods of high volatility for Chinese commodities are mostly bull markets, and reducing positions based on fluctuations is equivalent to reducing positions in a bull market.

Volatility is very predictable: the higher the volatility last month, the higher the volatility next month. The key point of the paper is that when volatility is high, the average return does not correspondingly increase, so the "return per risk taken" becomes worse, and you should hold less. The CSI 300 fits this: the group with the highest volatility last month has a negative average return next month. The average commodity factor does not meet the requirements: the group with the lowest volatility last month will have a negative average return next month, and the strategy happens to add positions at this time.

3. Why is the product invalid?

  • The effective part of the stock index is the short-term deviation: When the stock market suddenly becomes more turbulent than usual (such as the 2015 stock market crash), things will often get worse next, and you can avoid it by reducing your positions in time. When CSI 500 only uses this part, α reaches 11% (t=3.2), but the long-term level part is -9%. The two cancel out, so the paper approach seems invalid on CSI 500.
  • Commodity losses come from long-term levels: The low-volatility periods of Chinese commodities are concentrated in 2013-2014, 2018-2019, and 2025, and most of them are slow declines or sideways; the high-volatility periods are concentrated in 2008-2009, 2016-2017, and 2021-2022. Except for 2008, they are mostly bull markets. To reduce positions according to fluctuations is equivalent to increasing positions in a slow bear market and reducing positions in a bull market. When using only short-term deviations, the commodity neither earns nor loses (-0.3%).
  • On an annual basis, the correlation between commodities with "high volatility and high returns in that year" is only +0.15 (20 years), which is not statistically strong. The above is an explanation consistent with the data, rather than strong evidence. In addition, we have checked that the variance is low due to fewer trading days in holiday months and a few extreme months, but they are not the main reasons.

As in the paper, using volatility instead of variance and AR(1) to predict variance can significantly reduce extreme leverage (the 99th percentile drops from about 5 times to 2.5~3 times), and α is still positive; after increasing the leverage upper limit of 1 or 1.5, α becomes smaller but Sharpe basically remains unchanged. The breakeven cost of CSI 300 is about 100bp per unit turnover, which is much higher than the actual transaction cost of stock index futures (about 1~2bp). Cost is not an issue.

Conclusion

  • Established on stock index: CSI 300 After volatility management, α 6.4%, Sharpe increased by about half, consistent with the paper; what works is to reduce positions when short-term fluctuations suddenly amplify.
  • Not true on the product: Commodity average factor and commodity carry have not improved or even worsened after volatility management. The reason is that periods of high volatility for Chinese commodities are mostly bull markets, and reducing positions based on the level of volatility is equivalent to reducing positions in a bull market; timing only based on short-term deviations will result in neither profit nor loss.
  • The effect is concentrated in a small number of periods: The α of the stock index mainly comes from the period of great fluctuations around 2015, and has not been significant in recent years.

Differences from the paper

  • Factors are different: China does not have long-term, public stock factor daily returns. Here we use stock indexes, treasury bonds, commodities on futures and TSMOM and carry reproduced in the previous two articles.
  • The sample is short: the stock index starts in 2010, the CSI 500 and treasury bonds start in 2015, and the commodity starts in 2005; the paper starts in 1926.
  • The recession period test, option realization, VAR and equilibrium model parts have not been done.
  • The supplementary “long-term level/short-term deviation” split is a new addition to the analysis and is not part of the paper.
