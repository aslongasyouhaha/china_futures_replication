Report on the recurrence of squeeze before delivery of Chinese commodity futures

Reproduction paper: Iosif Zimbidis & Ronnie Sircar，《Negative Oil & Nickel Squeeze: A Feedback Model for Extreme Commodity Futures Prices》, arXiv:2610.00951, 2026 · China Commodity Futures 2005–2026 All contract expirations · Daily and 5-minute data

The paper's feedback model is mathematically reproduced in full (option values are consistent with WTI's implied imbalance of 0.87). The mechanism described in the paper does exist in China: individual customers must close their positions before the delivery month. In this month, the deviation of the nearby-contract from the next month is significantly greater than that of the previous month (the deviation exceeds 10% of expiration 145 times vs. 18 times), which is mainly depressed, and concentrated in the last two or three days before the closing deadline. However, the magnitude of China's deviation is much smaller than extreme events such as crude oil falling into negative numbers and nickel prices doubling; the method used in the paper to infer the "squeeze degree" relies on the assumption of normal prices, which is only suitable for explaining individual events and is not suitable for systematic comparisons.

What does the paper talk about

On April 20, 2020, the WTI crude oil May contract fell to −$37.63 the day before expiration; on March 8, 2022, the LME nickel price rose to more than $100,000 in one day. The paper believes that the two have a common mechanism: some people in the market (financial institutions, speculators) cannot make physical delivery and must close their positions before expiration. If these people are net long, they must concentrate on selling (crude oil, nearby-contract is pushed down, Case 1); if they are net short, they must concentrate on buying (nickel, nearby-contract is pushed up, Case 2). There are only a few opponents who can deliver, and the price is distorted.

2. Copying the back-extraction method: looking at each event one by one is unreliable

For 5930 contract expirations, on the day when the deviation is the largest in the stress month, ε can be deduced according to equation (57):

situationExpiration times25% / median / 75% quartile of ε
The extrusion direction is in the same direction as the original price difference (crude oil and nickel both fall into this category)28190.39 / 0.54 / 0.70
opposite direction31110.80 / 0.96 / 1.00

The inverse formula is ε = deviation ÷ (deviation + sub-month price × option value). When the direction is opposite, the option is out-of-the-money, and the value approaches zero near expiration. A small deviation brings ε close to 1, with no distinction; even if the direction is the same, the method attributes all changes in the spread to forced closing. Therefore, this method is suitable for answering "how much liquidation pressure is required to explain an extreme price", but is not suitable for comparison between a large number of events.

  • Benchmark A: the ratio before the beginning of the stress month; Benchmark B: the ratio at the opening of the first day of the last week (the paper's approach). The same event ε varies greatly with the baseline, which is a limitation acknowledged by the paper itself.
  • When the direction is consistent with the original price difference, the ε (0.90) of low-sulfur fuel oil is of the same magnitude as crude oil and nickel; beans and soda ash are smaller.
  • Soda ash and WTI were in the same place in April 2020. Both saw a sharp drop in demand and a backlog of inventories. The soda ash nearby-contract was only depressed by about 12%, and there was no negative price. In the same month, the ratio of Shanghai crude oil SC2005 to SC2006 only dropped from 0.977 to 0.94.
  • Counter example: The original price difference of coking coal JM2201 (2021-12) is at a discount and the nearby-contract is depressed. The option is out-of-value and ε has been close to 1; and the deviation has been formed in the middle of the month, which is more like a normal convergence driven by the sharp drop in coal prices.

Conclusion

  • model: Mathematically complete reproduction. It can explain "how much liquidation pressure is required to produce such a price", but the inverse ε relies on the assumption of normal prices and cannot be used as a measurement of the true position imbalance.
  • Mechanism exists in China: In the month when individual positions are forced to be liquidated, the nearby-contract deviation is significantly larger, mainly driven down, concentrated in the last two or three days before the deadline, and is more obvious in instruments where delivery is difficult.
  • The extent is much smaller than extreme events overseas: The largest deviation in China is less than 30%, with no negative prices. It may be because individuals are required to withdraw as a whole one month in advance, and the pressure is dispersed; the exchange increases margins and limits positions for contracts approaching delivery (speculation, not verified).
  • Connection to three-factor study: The momentum and carry are particularly strong when holding a nearby-contract according to the paper's method. Part of this may come from the price distortion caused by forced liquidation before delivery, and this part of the income cannot be obtained by individual investors according to the rules.

limitations

  • Before delivery, the near-contract would have converged to the spot; without spot data, it is impossible to completely separate the deviation caused by the forced liquidation from the normal basis convergence. The above evidence (time is concentrated before the cut-off, the direction is mainly downward, and related to the difficulty of delivery) supports the squeeze mechanism, but the role of basis convergence cannot be ruled out.
  • Without position data on “who can deliver,” ε cannot be measured independently.
  • The smaller the position decreases, the greater the deviation of the contract. The reason is unknown and may be related to the thin trading.
